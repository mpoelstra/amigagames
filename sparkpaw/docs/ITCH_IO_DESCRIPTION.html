A storm is waking in the ruins

Sparkpaw: The Stormstone Quest is an original side-scrolling action platformer and aerial shooter for the Commodore Amiga 1200. Play as Sparkpaw, an amber-orange feline adventurer and young inventor armed with a teal energy gauntlet. Explore ancient stone-and-machine ruins, recover the first Stormstone Core, then take to the skies aboard the Skimmer.

The current alpha offers two connected playable sections: Storm Ruins and Stormrail. Run, jump and fight through a sprawling platforming route, then steer through enemy formations, storm-lit valleys and a debris field toward the Harrier boss. Original Amiga music accompanies the adventure, with music and sound effects playing together throughout the action.

Explore Storm Ruins

Cross twelve scrolling screens of platforms, water hazards and ruined machinery beneath a distant storm-lit citadel. Fight Clockwork Beetles and towering Storm Striders, collect diamonds and health, search for a hidden extra life, and reach the Stormkeeper's Waystation and the first Stormstone Core.

  • Running, variable-height jumping, crouching, crouch-walking and rapid plasma fire
  • Animated enemies, energy impacts, splashing water and ledge-balance poses
  • Layered AGA scenery with distant mountains, waterfalls, ruins and parallax scrolling
  • Health, lives, collectibles and a score tally for enemies, diamonds and time

Board the Skimmer and fly Stormrail

Continue from Storm Ruins with your remaining lives, health and diamond meter. Jump into the waiting Skimmer and launch into a horizontal shooter section, flying through rain-veiled valleys and ruined structures as the storm gathers.

  • Storm Darts and Pulse Orbs attacking in sweeping, crossing and curling formations
  • Diamond-chain rewards for clearing formations, plus health pickups along the route
  • A debris field with destructible masonry, solid obstacles and hidden rewards
  • The Harrier finale: dodge spreading fan shots and aimed bursts, defeat the boss and fly through the opened gate
  • End-of-section statistics and near-instant replay for another attempt

Play the connected route or choose either section from OPTIONS on the ready screen.

A final chance in the storm

Alpha.7 introduces a dedicated GAME OVER screen when the last life is lost, with Sparkpaw resting defeated among the ruins, your total campaign score and Storm Light. Press Fire to return to the title. The same artwork and music are included in HD, WHDLoad and the two-disk ADF edition.

Your remaining lives and health carry into Stormrail and are shown from the boarding sequence onward. Starting Stormrail directly through OPTIONS gives you a fresh run with three lives and full health.

A soundtrack for the adventure

Five original themes give each part of Sparkpaw its own atmosphere: Hero Drive for the cinematic introduction, Neon Sky for the title and ready screens, Copper Sprint for Storm Ruins Iron Horizon for Stormrail and Storm Light for game over.

Gameplay music shares Paula's four hardware channels with plasma shots, jumps, pickups, splashes, debris impacts and boss attack cues. Three channels carry the music while a small software mixer combines two effect voices on the fourth. The intro and title themes use all four channels.

The ready-screen OPTIONS menu can set gameplay audio to SFX only, music only, or both. HD and WHDLoad editions also include a SOUNDTEST for all 16 sound effects and all five themes. These choices only affect Storm Ruins and Stormrail gameplay; the title and results retain their normal audio.

Built for the Amiga

Sparkpaw runs natively on the Amiga 1200, using AGA bitplanes, Copper colour changes, the Blitter, hardware sprites and Paula audio. Its presentation includes a 64-colour five-panel story introduction in the HD and WHDLoad editions, dedicated title and loading artwork, and an animated ready screen with drifting wind particles.

This is an in-development alpha. Storm Ruins and Stormrail are playable through their respective endings; later worlds and the rest of the planned adventure are still in development. Feedback is welcome, especially with your machine, accelerator, RAM and package type.

Requirements

  • PAL Commodore Amiga 1200 or compatible AGA system
  • 68020 or faster CPU
  • 2 MB Chip RAM and 8 MB Fast RAM
  • Joystick in port 2, or keyboard controls

Development testing includes FS-UAE at 68020 and 68030 speeds and a real A1200/68030. Results vary with the machine and available memory. For an ordinary HD launch, keep as much Chip RAM free as possible: Workbench and other software can leave too little for Stormrail even with 2 MB installed. Hardware testing of the current audio builds is ongoing.

Controls

  • Ready menu: Up/down selects; left/right changes values; Fire/Space confirms. OPTIONS selects your starting section, secondary joystick action and gameplay audio mode. HD/WHDLoad also provide SOUNDTEST.
  • On foot: Joystick left/right to run, up to jump, down to crouch and primary Fire to shoot. The secondary button defaults to Jump.
  • In flight: Steer the Skimmer with the joystick directions and use Fire to shoot.
  • Keyboard: A/D to move, W to jump, S to crouch and Space to shoot; W/A/S/D steer during flight. Press P to pause or resume gameplay.
  • Escape: Leave the current run and return to the ready screen.
  • Story intro: Fire reveals/advances; hold Fire or use the left mouse button to skip.
  • WHDLoad: F10 returns to Workbench.

Downloads

All editions contain Storm Ruins, Stormrail, gameplay music and sound effects.

  • HD: Extract the LHA or ZIP to a hard drive and launch Sparkpaw from its drawer. Includes the cinematic story introduction, all four music themes and SOUNDTEST.
  • WHDLoad: LHA and ZIP packages for an existing WHDLoad installation, with the cinematic introduction, all five themes and SOUNDTEST. WHDLoad and Kickstart files are not included.
  • Two-disk ADF: Two ordinary 880 KiB floppy images. Boot Disk 1 for the title and Storm Ruins; Disk 2 contains Stormrail. Use disk swaps in a single drive, or place Disk 2 in DF1 for automatic detection. The floppy edition includes title and gameplay music, audio mode, loading and ready screens, but omits the cinematic story introduction and SOUNDTEST.

Use both ADFs from the same release. Choose HD or WHDLoad for the complete story presentation.

Development: MrDig + AI, one test at a time

Sparkpaw is created through a collaboration between MrDig and AI coding agent Codex. MrDig directs the game, art and sound, judges how it feels to play, and tests builds in emulation and on real hardware. Codex helps implement the engine, create assets and tools, analyse test results and document the work.

Sparkpaw by the numbers

The most recent measured development snapshot was taken on 9 September 2026, at the alpha.5 checkpoint:

  • Five weeks from the first public repository baseline on 5 August to this snapshot
  • 83 Sparkpaw commits across 19 separate commit dates
  • 663 preserved evidence files: 125 gameplay/test videos, 136 logs, 108 screenshots and other images, and 294 written reports and evidence sidecars
  • 449 archived build drawers containing both a ReadMe and a native Amiga executable, including A/B variants, accepted references and rejected experiments
  • 89 local build/work directories for packaging, diagnostics, generated assets and experiments
  • 9 music-experiment directories and 2 dedicated audio proof projects, exploring composition, replay, mixing and gameplay integration
  • About 33,000 lines of C and Python, plus 22,000 lines of documentation and written test material, excluding bundled third-party code
  • Four original music themes, 16 sound effects and just four Paula hardware channels to share
  • Two playable sections, packaged as HD and WHDLoad downloads and two ordinary 880 KiB floppy images
  • 15 KiB free on Disk 1 and 159 KiB on Disk 2, with gameplay music and effects included

These are archive counts, not a count of unique bugs, independent tests or distinct game versions. Build drawers include variants and preserved copies; a day without a commit may still contain hours of playtesting. The earlier publication snapshot estimated 80–120 combined development and testing hours. Considerable work has followed, but there is no timesheet to support an honest updated total.

The project grows through playable experiments, recorded evidence and plenty of time spent with an actual Amiga. For the development story, release details and occasional dives into the custom chips, see the devlog.
